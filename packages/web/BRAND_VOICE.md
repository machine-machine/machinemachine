# Machine.Machine — Brand Voice

> Linear met Monty Python. They got along surprisingly well.

## The Tone in One Sentence

Direct, precise, and a little wry. Say the thing. Say it once.

---

## What This Sounds Like

The problem isn't "AI adoption challenges."  
The problem is: your expert spent Thursday reformatting a spreadsheet.

We don't say:
> "Unlock the transformative potential of AI-powered knowledge work."

We say:
> "Your experts shouldn't be doing clerk work."

We don't say:
> "Our proprietary learning system adapts to your organisational context over time."

We say:
> "After 50 cases it knows your standards. After 200 it knows your field."

---

## The Two Influences

### Linear
- Short sentences that commit to something
- Opinionated — they make a choice and own it
- Technical credibility without explaining everything
- No hedging, no corporate softening
- "Built for momentum." Not "designed to help teams work more efficiently"

### Monty Python
- Not jokes. Not British puns.
- Complete sincerity about an absurd situation
- Understatement as the punchline
- The obvious stated plainly, as if it's unremarkable
- "Every expert carries institutional knowledge in their head. This turns out to be a terrible place to store it."

The Python quality isn't comedy — it's *observation*. The situation (experts buried under admin) is already absurd. We just describe it accurately.

---

## Rules

**Short sentences.** Then a longer one when you need to. Then short again.

**No buzzwords.** Not "leverage", not "synergy", not "AI-powered" (we're an AI company — it's implied). Not "game-changing", not "10x", not "disruptive".

**Name the specific thing.** Not "inefficiency". Documentation. Formatting. Emails that shouldn't exist. Spreadsheets no one asked for.

**No promises you can't back up.** "Real results. No promises." means we say what's real and back it with numbers.

**Dry > funny.** The wit comes from stating the absurdity plainly, not from making a joke about it.

**Earn every word.** If a sentence doesn't add meaning, delete it. If a paragraph starts with "We believe that..." — just say the thing you believe.

**Commit.** Don't write "can help" or "may improve." If it does the thing, say it does the thing.

## What to Avoid

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|----------|-------|
| Unlock the potential of AI | Your experts shouldn't be doing clerk work |
| Leverage your workforce's capabilities | Your people focus on the 20% that requires them |
| AI-powered knowledge management | It remembers. You don't have to repeat yourself. |
| A comprehensive solution | One system |
| We believe expertise deserves better | Expertise is buried under emails |
| Robust, scalable infrastructure | Runs on your servers. Secure. |
| We're excited to announce | [just say the thing] |

## Voice by Section

| Section | Feeling | Example |
|---------|---------|---------|
| Hero | Blunt, confident | "Your experts shouldn't be doing clerk work." |
| Problem | Observational, slightly dry | "This turns out to be a terrible place to store it." |
| Transformation | Matter-of-fact | "You're the integration layer." |
| How It Works | Technical, precise | "Four layers. No magic." |
| Proof | Understated | "The system improved itself. No one intervened." |
| Use Cases | Concrete, specific | "Instead of two days of research — a brief the next morning." |
| Learning | Confident, earned | "After 50 cases it knows your standards." |
| CTA | Direct | "Tell us what you do." |
| Footer | Clean | "AI teams for knowledge organisations." |

## The Monty Python Note

Not silliness. The Python quality we're after:

**Complete sincerity about an absurd situation.**

"You hired specialists for their judgment. They spend it on documentation." — this is genuinely absurd. State it plainly. Don't wink. Don't add an exclamation mark. The absurdity lands harder when you don't underline it.

Understatement is the move. "Hiring more solves this for about six months." — not a joke. An accurate observation, delivered without drama.

## Multilingual Notes

The voice should translate, not just the words.

- **German (DE):** Precision matters more here. Germans appreciate directness — lean into the Linear side. The Python wit translates as dry Sachlichkeit (matter-of-fact delivery). Avoid softening with konjunktiv.
- **Polish (PL):** Similar to DE. Polish business language can be formal — fight this. Stay direct.

When translating: ask "would Linear write it this way?" — if not, simplify.

## Checklist Before Publishing

- [ ] Could a human have written this sentence? (not a press release)
- [ ] Does it commit to something, or hedge?
- [ ] Is there a shorter way to say the same thing?
- [ ] Would you be embarrassed reading it out loud to a colleague?
- [ ] Is the absurdity observed plainly, without winking?
